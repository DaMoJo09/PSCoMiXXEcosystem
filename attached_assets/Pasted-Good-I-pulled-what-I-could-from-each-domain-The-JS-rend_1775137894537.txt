🧠 CROSS-CUTTING ANALYSIS
What Would Make This a $100M Product
Here’s the honest answer: the pieces exist. The problem is architecture, not vision.
The $100M version of this looks like:
	1.	One brand, one entry point. pressstart.space becomes the authenticated portal. Everything else becomes a subdomain: create.pressstart.space, learn.pressstart.space, stream.pressstart.space. The multi-domain strategy fragments SEO, trust, and user journeys across five separate buckets.
	2.	The LMS is the Trojan Horse. Schools adopt the LMS. The LMS feeds students into CoMiXX. CoMiXX outputs to the Streaming Platform. XP ties it all together. That loop is your flywheel. Right now it’s five separate tools with no visible flywheel.
	3.	Teachers are your actual user. Not students. Teachers choose the tools. Teachers approve the budget. Teachers submit grants. Every product surface needs a teacher-facing entry point. Currently zero of the five products have an obvious “I’m a teacher, start here” path.
	4.	The nonprofit/for-profit structure is an asset — if used correctly. Press Start Gaming Inc. should be the grant-facing entity. MADMIXEDMEDIA LLC should be the licensing and services entity. They should almost never appear on the same page together publicly.
	5.	The “One Script → Three Outputs” pipeline is genuinely innovative for a K-12 context. It’s not explained anywhere a non-insider can find it.
	6.	Fix the JS-only renders before any investor pitch. SSR or static generation for at minimum the marketing shells of pressstart.tech and psstreaming.com. You cannot pitch a product that shows a blank page.
The Real Competitive Moat
The curriculum alignment (California CTE standards), the Reallusion/Wacom/Epic partnerships, and the existing classroom deployment experience are things a startup VC would fund. None of them are visible on any of these five sites. That’s the gap between a $1M grant and a $10M round.
