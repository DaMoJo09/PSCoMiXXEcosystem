📝 Week 01 · Script
WRITE THE
WORLD

Every great visual starts with a great line. Students open the Script mode and learn how to draft for four formats — Comic, Screenplay, Novel, Kids Book — then concept their story title and pitch the universe in 60 seconds.

5 SessionsMon–Fri, 55 min each
Modes UsedScript · Title
DeliverableStory Bible + Title Concepts
Skill LevelBeginner — No prior experience
📝
pscomixx.online/script · pscomixx.online/title

Write the script first. Name the world. Pitch it out loud. Visuals come next — and they come faster when the script is solid.

Learning Objectives

  • Open and navigate the Script mode in FX Studio
  • Write in Comic format using panel + caption structure
  • Compare Comic, Screenplay, Novel, and Kids Book modes
  • Concept a title and design first-pass title cards
  • Deliver a 60-second story pitch out loud

Teacher Setup Notes

  • Pre-load pscomixx.online on student devices and verify login
  • Prepare 2–3 short comic page scripts as printed examples
  • Have students enable autosave; show them where drafts live
  • Print the "4 Script Formats Compared" reference (Session 1)
  • Plan time for 3–4 students to pitch live each Friday

01.1 Why Write First — The 4 Script Modes Tour LECTURE + DEMO 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Open with two side-by-side comic pages: one drafted from a script, one improvised. Ask: "Which one tells the story cleanly?" Walk through the four Script mode formats — Comic, Screenplay, Novel, Kids Book — and show what each one is for. Students draft a 1-page test in any format.

Timing (Teacher View)
0:00Hook — script-vs-improv comparison projected (5 min)
0:05Direct instruction — the 4 formats with examples (15 min)
0:20Live demo — teacher writes a 4-panel scene in Comic mode (10 min)
0:30Student practice — pick a format, draft 1 page (20 min)
0:50Share-out — 3 volunteers read their drafts aloud (5 min)
Student Activity

Open the Script mode at pscomixx.online/script. Pick any format (Comic, Screenplay, Novel, or Kids Book). Write 1 page about a character making one important choice. Save your draft. In your creator log, write one sentence: which format felt most natural to you, and why?

THE SCRIPT IS THE CHEAPEST PART OF THE COMIC. WASTE IT EARLY.
Resources
  • 4 Script Formats Compared — printable reference card
  • FX Studio Script mode quick-start guide
  • Sample 1-page comic script (PDF)
01.2 Comic Script Format — Beats, Panels, Captions WRITING LAB 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Deep dive on the Comic format. Vocabulary: BEAT, PANEL, CAPTION, BALLOON, SFX. Students study a real one-shot script, then draft a 6-panel scene of their own following the structure. Emphasis: every panel must do one job.

Student Activity

Draft a 6-panel comic scene in Script mode. Each panel needs: a one-line action description, who is in it, and the dialogue or caption. Number every panel. Save it. Re-read it — could an artist draw this without asking you a single question?

Timing (Teacher View)
0:00Vocab review — beat / panel / caption / balloon / SFX (10 min)
0:10Read-aloud — sample one-shot script projected (10 min)
0:20Student draft — 6-panel scene in Comic format (30 min)
0:50Pair swap — partner reads, asks one question (5 min)
01.3 Screenplay & Novel Modes — Choosing the Right Frame WRITING LAB 55 min ▶
Session Summary

The same scene — written three ways. Students take their Day 2 comic scene and re-write it once in Screenplay format and once in Novel format using the FX Studio mode switcher. Discussion: what changes? What stays the same? When would you pick each format?

Student Activity

Take your 6-panel comic from yesterday. Switch the Script mode to Screenplay format and rewrite the scene. Then switch to Novel format and rewrite it again. Save all three drafts side-by-side. Which version pulls you in the hardest? In your log, explain why.

01.4 Title Card Concepts — Name Your Universe BRAND DESIGN 55 min ▶
Session Summary

First touch of the Title mode. A great title is a promise. Students brainstorm 10 candidate titles for their story, then test the top 3 in the Title mode using different display fonts and FX treatments — no full design yet, just concept cards.

Student Activity

Brainstorm 10 possible titles for your story. Pick your top 3. Open pscomixx.online/title. Build a quick-pass title card for each — pick a font, pick a color, hit one effect (glow, shadow, distress). Save all three. Which one makes a stranger want to read your comic?

A TITLE IS A PROMISE. BREAK IT AND THEY WALK.
01.5 Pitch Day — 60-Second Story Pitch CRITIQUE + PITCH 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Every student pitches their story in 60 seconds: title, world, main character, central conflict. Class gives warm feedback. Teacher anchors the practice in industry: this is exactly how comics get pitched to publishers and platforms.

Timing (Teacher View)
0:00Pitch structure mini-lesson — title / world / hero / problem (10 min)
0:10Pair rehearsal — each student pitches to a partner twice (10 min)
0:20Live pitches — 60 sec each, class of 20 ≈ 30 min (30 min)
0:50Closing — class names 3 favorite story hooks (5 min)

STORY BIBLE + 3 TITLE CONCEPTS

You now have a written world, three possible names for it, and a pitch you can deliver out loud. Everything else this course builds sits on top of this.

  • One 6-panel scene drafted in Comic Script format
  • Same scene re-written in Screenplay and Novel formats
  • 3 title concepts saved in the Title mode
  • 60-second pitch delivered (live or recorded)
  • Creator reflection — which format and which title felt right?

02.1 Character Creator — Sketch / Chibi / Creature Styles TOOL INTRO 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Full walkthrough of the Character mode — Sketch, Chibi, and Creature style families. Students learn the layer system (silhouette → base → costume → accessory → expression) and build their first hero in the style that fits their story.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/character. Pick a style family that matches your story's vibe. Build your hero from the silhouette out: body type, costume, signature accessory, default expression. Export a transparent PNG and save it to the Asset Lab. This is your protagonist.

SILHOUETTE FIRST. IF I CAN'T NAME THEM IN BLACK, I CAN'T NAME THEM IN COLOR.
02.2 Portrait Mode — Headshots, Expressions, Tints DESIGN LAB 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Portraits are emotional shorthand. Students take their hero into the Portrait mode and build a 4-expression set: Calm, Angry, Sad, Surprised. They learn how color tint and lighting on a portrait change the emotional read more than the expression itself.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/portrait and pull in your hero. Build 4 expression portraits — Calm, Angry, Sad, Surprised. Then pick one and tint it 3 ways: warm tint (hopeful), cool tint (sad), red tint (rage). Save all variants. These become your reaction shots.

02.3 Build Your Cast — Hero, Foil, Wildcard PRODUCTION 55 min ▶
Session Summary

A story needs more than a hero. Students design two more characters in the Character mode: a FOIL (the opposite of the hero) and a WILDCARD (a character you can't predict). Same Character + Portrait pipeline, applied across the cast.

Student Activity

Build two more characters: a FOIL and a WILDCARD. Each gets a full Character Creator pass + at least 2 Portrait expressions. By end of session, you should have 3 transparent character PNGs and 6+ portrait variants saved. This is your cast sheet.

02.4 Bubble Editor 101 — Voice Through Shape TYPE DESIGN 55 min ▶
Session Summary

First touch of the Bubble mode. Round = friendly. Jagged = shouting. Cloud = thought. Rectangular caption = narrator. Students give each of their 3 characters a signature bubble style and a signature font, then place one line of dialogue from each into a sample frame.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/bubble. Assign each character a signature bubble style (shape) and font (voice). Drop one line of dialogue per character — straight from your Week 1 script. Save each as a transparent bubble PNG. Stack the three bubbles in one test layout. Can you tell who's talking just by the bubble shape?

THE BUBBLE IS NOT DECORATION. IT'S VOICE.
02.5 Cast Sheet Review CRITIQUE 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Each student presents their 3-character cast sheet: name, one-line bio, signature bubble + line of dialogue. Class gives one strength + one suggestion per cast. Teacher connects the practice to industry "model sheets" used by every animation and comic studio.

3-CHARACTER CAST SHEET

You have a hero, a foil, and a wildcard. Each one looks like themselves. Each one talks like themselves. Your story has people in it now.

  • 3 characters built in Character mode — exported as transparent PNGs
  • 4 expression portraits per character (12 total minimum)
  • 1 signature bubble + dialogue line per character
  • Cast sheet — names, 1-line bios, hero pose for each
  • Creator reflection — who is your favorite character to write?

03.1 Filter Editor — The Stack TOOL INTRO 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Filters are not Instagram presets — in FX Studio they STACK. Students learn the filter library (halftone, glitch, posterize, ink wash, scanline, color shift) and discover how stacking 2–3 filters at different opacities creates entirely new looks.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/filter. Drop in your hero portrait. Apply ONE filter. Then add a second. Then a third — different opacity each time. Build 4 different stacks. Export a strip of "the same character, four looks." Which look feels like YOUR story?

A FILTER IS NOT A SETTING. IT'S A SENTENCE.
03.2 Graffiti Mode — Spray, Drip, Stencil CREATIVE LAB 55 min ▶
Session Summary

The Graffiti mode is a digital spray paint canvas with drip physics and stencil layers. Students learn how to use spray, drip, and stencil to create hand-drawn FX — tags, marks, signatures, energy bursts, blood splatters, paint splashes — that no preset filter can fake.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/graffiti. Pick a stencil shape (star burst, heart, splash, drip). Spray it 3 different ways: fast (light), slow (heavy), with pulled drips. Then make ONE custom mark — your signature tag, your hero's emblem, or a story symbol. Save them all as transparent PNGs.

03.3 FX Studio Main Engine — Layered FX & Motion Stamps FX LAB 55 min ▶
Session Summary

The main FX engine is where animated overlays live — motion stamps, sound FX bursts, glow flares, smoke plumes, light leaks. Students stack 2–3 FX layers on a test panel and learn how layer order changes the read of the scene.

Student Activity

Open the main FX studio. Drop your hero portrait on the canvas. Stack 3 FX layers: one weather (rain/smoke), one motion (speed lines/burst), one light (glow/flare). Reorder them — see how the front layer dominates. Export your favorite stack. Save it as your "house FX recipe."

03.4 Style Pass — Lock Your Look PRODUCTION 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Locking the look. Students pick their favorite filter stack + favorite graffiti mark + favorite FX recipe and apply them as a single house style across all 3 characters and 1 sample background. Save the recipe to the Asset Lab so it's reusable for the rest of the course.

Student Activity

Apply your locked style to your full cast. Export 3 character portraits using the same filter stack, the same graffiti accent, and the same FX recipe. Then save the recipe in the Asset Lab tagged "house-style." From now on, this is what your story looks like.

03.5 Mid-Course Look Book Review CRITIQUE 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Mid-course milestone. Each student displays a 5-image Look Book: 3 styled characters + 1 styled background + 1 close-up of their custom graffiti mark. Class scores using the rubric. Teacher conferences with anyone whose look isn't reading as consistent yet.

STYLE LOOK BOOK + LOCKED FILTER RECIPE

This is the mid-point check. Your story has a face now — a consistent filter stack, a custom mark, and a signature FX recipe that lives in the Asset Lab.

  • 5-image Look Book — 3 characters + 1 background + 1 graffiti mark
  • Locked filter recipe saved to Asset Lab as "house-style"
  • 1 custom graffiti mark unique to your story
  • 1 named FX recipe (e.g., "rain + neon + flare")
  • Creator reflection — what does your world FEEL like now?

04.1 BG FX Builder — Speed Lines, Starfields, Halftone TOOL INTRO 55 min ▶
Session Summary

BG FX is a procedural background generator: speed lines, starfields, halftone fields, radial bursts, gradient skies. Students learn the parameter controls (density, angle, color) and generate a small library of backgrounds tagged by mood: ACTION, CALM, COSMIC, NOIR.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/bgfx. Generate at least 4 backgrounds, one per mood category: ACTION (speed lines / radial burst), CALM (gradient sky / soft halftone), COSMIC (starfield / nebula), NOIR (dense halftone / dark gradient). Save all 4 to the Asset Lab. These are your story's worlds.

YOU DON'T NEED TO TRAVEL. YOU NEED TO GENERATE.
04.2 Overlay Mode — Transparent FX Layers FX LAB 55 min ▶
Session Summary

The Overlay mode produces transparent FX layers that drop on top of any panel — rain sheets, smoke plumes, lens flares, dust particles, bokeh, paper distress. Students learn the workflow: build the overlay alone first (transparent canvas), then drop it onto an existing scene.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/overlay. Build 3 overlay layers — one weather (rain or snow), one light (lens flare or god rays), one texture (dust grain or paper distress). Each one must be saved as a transparent PNG. Then drop all 3 on top of one of your BG FX backgrounds. The world should now feel ALIVE.

04.3 Layout Editor — Page Spreads at 300 DPI LAYOUT LAB 55 min ▶
Session Summary

The Layout editor is the page-spread workspace at print resolution (300 DPI). Students learn the panel-grid system, gutter spacing, bleed area, and the difference between single-page and double-page layouts. They lay out their first real page using their cast + backgrounds.

Student Activity

Open pscomixx.online/layout. Pick a 6-panel layout template. Drop in: your hero (Wk 2) + a BG FX background (today) + an overlay (today). Don't add dialogue yet — just compose the spread visually. Export a low-res preview. Print it if you can. Tape it to the wall.

04.4 World Pass — Backgrounds × Overlays × Layout PRODUCTION 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Full production day. Students build 3 complete spreads using the BG FX → Overlay → Layout pipeline, applying their Week 3 house style to keep the look consistent. Teacher floats and conferences. Goal: by end of session, every student has 3 full layout spreads exported as PNG.

Student Activity

Build 3 page spreads today. Each spread = BG FX background + at least 1 overlay + your cast + your locked filter recipe. Each spread should be from a different beat in your script: Opening, Middle, Cliffhanger. Export all three. These are real comic pages.

04.5 World Critique — Does It Feel Real? CRITIQUE 55 min ▶
Session Summary

Gallery walk. Students post their 3 spreads side-by-side. Class moves through the room with sticky notes giving structured feedback: "This world feels ___" and "I want to see more of ___." Teacher anchors the critique on continuity: do the 3 spreads belong to the same world?

3-SPREAD WORLD SET

You have a real world now — backgrounds you generated, overlays you placed, panels you composed. Three spreads that prove your comic exists in space.

  • 4 BG FX backgrounds tagged by mood in Asset Lab
  • 3 transparent overlay PNGs (weather, light, texture)
  • 3 layout spreads — opening, middle, cliffhanger
  • House style applied consistently across all 3 spreads
  • Creator reflection — what does your world look like from a stranger's eyes?
